01 Wellhead installation
01

Wellhead installation

IWS installs wellheads and casing heads end to end — setting and levelling the base, landing and tacking the casing head, welding it to the casing under a qualified procedure, and heat-treating the joint so the welded joint is completed to the applicable sour-service requirements and pressure-tight. Work is delivered onshore, offshore and on unconventional wells, to the rig's schedule.

What's involved

  • Set-up and levelling of base table and work platform at the rig site
  • Landing, alignment and tack of the casing head to the casing
  • Preheat, root, fill and cap passes to a qualified WPS
  • Controlled post-weld heat treatment and controlled cool-down
  • Blast, paint and hand-back with a full documented record
The IWS edge

Conventional installs rely on a single-shot exothermic heat source to hit temperature fast. IWS uses electrically-controlled heating with thermocouples and a calibrated chart recorder — so heating and cooling rates are held to specification and every weld leaves with a verifiable temperature record, not just a hand-back.

02 Workover
02

Workover

Workover support keeps producing and injection wells in service. IWS recovers and re-installs wellheads and casing heads, carries out casing-pipe butt welding and re-stubbing, and supports rig and platform interventions — onshore and offshore. Every weld that carries pressure or sour-service duty is qualified, heat-treated and documented like any other IWS joint.

What's involved

  • Wellhead and casing-head recovery, re-installation and re-termination
  • Casing-pipe butt welding and re-stubbing to a qualified weld map
  • Hot-tap and injectivity testing across casing strings where required
  • Offshore and onshore intervention support to platform and rig schedules
  • Controlled preheat and PWHT with a full documented record
The IWS edge

A workover weld has to meet the same sour-service limits as new equipment. IWS brings its NACE-qualified, chart-recorded welding and PWHT to recovery and re-installation — so a well returns to service to a documented standard, not a field shortcut.

03 NACE welding & PWHT
03

NACE-compliant welding & PWHT

The heart of what IWS does. In sour (H₂S) service, the finished weld — filler metal and heat-affected zone included — must retain the base metal's properties to resist sulphide stress cracking. We achieve that with low-hydrogen procedures, controlled preheat, and post-weld heat treatment that relieves residual stress and brings weld-zone hardness back below the NACE limit.

What's involved

  • Qualified WPS / PQR for each material and weld type
  • Thermocouple-verified preheat and inter-pass control
  • Electrical-resistance PWHT with multi-zone pad control
  • Calibrated chart-recorder log for every weld
  • Bend, macro, hardness and tensile testing to ASME IX & NACE
The IWS edge

Exothermic heaters raise temperature quickly but can't hold a constant rate or prove what happened. Our electrical method gives a constant, repeatable heating/soak/cool profile, leaves no soot, heats locally without stripping adjacent equipment, and produces an auditable graph — heat treatment you can verify, not just trust.

04 Cold cutting
04

Cold cutting

Cold cutting severs and bevels pipe and casing mechanically — with no flame and no heat-affected zone. A split-frame, air-driven cutter clamps to the pipe outside diameter and produces a lathe-type square or bevelled end ready for welding. Because there's no ignition source, it's safe in live and hydrocarbon environments, onshore and offshore.

What's involved

  • Split-frame cutter mounts directly to the pipe O.D.
  • Square or bevelled cuts across a wide range of pipe and casing sizes
  • No open-flame cutting — safe near live equipment
  • Minimal radial and axial clearance required
  • Fast set-up, typically operational within minutes
Why it matters

Flame cutting introduces heat, alters the metallurgy at the cut, and brings an ignition source onto the well. Cold cutting leaves the base metal's properties untouched and the bevel weld-ready — which is exactly what NACE-compliant welding demands of the joint that follows.

05 Hot tapping
05

Hot tapping

Hot tapping makes a new connection into a pipe, vessel or line that stays live and under pressure — no shutdown, no loss of production. A drilling machine cuts through the wall while a valve isolates the tap, allowing new branches, instrument connections or completion fittings to be added safely.

What's involved

  • Connections made without shutting in the well or line
  • Tapping across a range of branch sizes
  • Isolation valve set so the tap is controlled throughout
  • Can be paired with controlled welding of the fitting
  • Delivered under procedure with full risk control
The IWS edge

Where a hot tap requires a welded branch or reinforcement, IWS brings the same NACE-qualified, chart-recorded welding to the connection — so the tap isn't just made live, it's made to a documented sour-service standard.

06 Quality testing & inspection
06

Quality testing & inspection

A weld is only finished when its quality is proven. IWS verifies the welds it makes — first by pressure-testing the welded section to confirm the joint is leak-tight, then by a documented inspection regime that examines the weld for surface and sub-surface flaws and checks weld-zone hardness against the sour-service limit. The focus is the integrity of the weld itself, not a full wellhead-system test.

Pressure testing — welded section

Pressure testing here is applied to the welded joint, not the whole wellhead system — it confirms that the weld IWS has made holds. The new or repaired section is isolated and brought up to the specified test pressure, then held for the code-required duration to expose any leak before the well returns to service.

  • Test applied to the welded joint / section, isolated from the wider wellhead
  • Hydrostatic or pneumatic test to the governing construction code (ASME B31.3 / ASME BPVC Section VIII)
  • Held at the specified test pressure for the code-required duration
  • Pressure-decay monitoring and leak detection across the weld
  • Result recorded against the code acceptance criteria

Quality inspection — MPI, UT & hardness

Each weld is examined non-destructively and checked for hardness by technicians qualified to ASNT SNT-TC-1A or ISO 9712, with results assessed against the applicable code acceptance criteria.

  • Magnetic particle inspection (MPI / MT) for surface and near-surface flaws — to ASME BPVC Section V Article 7 / ISO 17638
  • Ultrasonic testing (UT) for sub-surface and volumetric flaws — to ASME BPVC Section V Article 4 / ISO 17640
  • Hardness testing of the weld and heat-affected zone — Vickers to ASTM E92 / ISO 6507
  • Weld-zone hardness verified below the NACE sour-service limit (≤ the applicable NACE MR0175 / ISO 15156 and client hardness limit) per NACE MR0175 / ISO 15156
  • Full inspection record compiled and handed to the operator
Measured, not assumed

Testing the weld itself — not just the assembled wellhead — is what proves the joint IWS made is sound. Every pressure, NDT and hardness result is recorded against its code acceptance criteria and handed over as an auditable record, so weld quality is demonstrated by measurement, not assumed.

07 Repairs & fabrication

Repairs & fabrication

Beyond installation, IWS repairs wellhead and casing-head equipment in the field and fabricates to spec — skids, frames, supports and made-to-drawing assemblies — plus rig modifications and workover support. Wherever a weld carries pressure or sour-service duty, it's qualified, heat-treated and documented like any other IWS joint.

What's involved

  • In-field wellhead and casing-head repair and remediation
  • Custom fabrication built to drawing and welded to code
  • Rig modifications, upgrades and structural work
  • Offshore and onshore workover support
  • Qualified welders, inspectors and NDT/PWHT technicians
The IWS edge

Repair welds in sour service are only as good as their heat treatment. IWS restores integrity with the same controlled, chart-recorded PWHT used on new installs — so a repair meets the same NACE limits as original equipment, not a lower field standard.

Note: Every IWS service line is delivered to the same documented, code-compliant standard — qualified procedures, controlled preheat and chart-recorded PWHT, and full inspection records on every weld that carries pressure or sour-service duty.
